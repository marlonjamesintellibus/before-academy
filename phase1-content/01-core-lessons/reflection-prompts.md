Shared rules: reflections exist for consolidation (generation effect) and give testing sessions a window into mental models. They are never graded, never block progression, and receive non-judgmental acknowledgment — no correctness verdicts on free text. Surfacing rule (A5): at most one pool prompt per lesson block; P1-REF-001 always appears after the activity summary.

**Shared save behaviour:** signed-in learners' responses save to their profile and are visible to them later; guest responses live in the session and carry the standard guest-progress caveat. A visible "Saved" state confirms capture. Skipping leaves no trace and is never mentioned again.

**Shared accessibility note:** each prompt is a labelled multiline text field with a Save response button and a Skip option; the saved confirmation is announced by screen readers; no character minimum, no timer.

### P1-REF-001 — Daily-system reflection *(CS §5.6 required)*
- **Placement:** after the Sort the System summary.
- **Prompt:** Think of one system you use daily. Which category would you guess — and what would you need to know to be sure?
- **Response guidance (optional disclosure):** Name the system, your best-guess label, and one piece of evidence you'd want. Two or three sentences is plenty.
- **Example response:** "My banking app — probably a combination. The balance screen must be rules, but I'd want to know whether the fraud alerts are learned patterns or fixed limits."
- **Acknowledgment:** Saved. Asking what you'd need to know is exactly the habit this section is about.

### P1-REF-002 — Rule-based software you use
- **Placement:** end of the traditional-software lesson.
- **Prompt:** Name one piece of software you used today that's almost certainly rule-based. What's your evidence?
- **Response guidance:** Evidence usually sounds like "it does the same thing every time" or "the criteria are listed."
- **Example response:** "The parking meter app — same fee for the same zone and time, every time. That's a written price table."
- **Acknowledgment:** Saved. "Same input, same output" is the strongest evidence there is.

### P1-REF-003 — An automated process around you
- **Placement:** end of the automation lesson.
- **Prompt:** Think of one process in your life that runs without you — a bill, a backup, a reminder. What's the trigger?
- **Response guidance:** Triggers are usually a date, an event, or a threshold.
- **Example response:** "My phone backs up photos every night when it's charging — the trigger is the time plus being plugged in."
- **Acknowledgment:** Saved. Spotting the trigger is spotting the automation.

### P1-REF-004 — A product you assumed was AI
- **Placement:** end of the artificial-intelligence lesson.
- **Prompt:** Pick a product you always assumed was AI. What's your actual evidence — and what would you need to check?
- **Response guidance:** "It's impressive" and "the ads say so" both count as things to notice, not evidence.
- **Example response:** "My robot vacuum. Honestly my evidence was the word 'smart' on the box. I'd want to know if it maps rooms from learned patterns or follows bump-and-turn rules."
- **Acknowledgment:** Saved. Separating the label from the mechanism is the whole skill — well spotted either way.

### P1-REF-005 — Explain it to someone
- **Placement:** after the takeaway block.
- **Prompt:** How would you explain the difference between automation and AI to a friend in two sentences?
- **Response guidance:** Try one sentence for how work flows, one for how decisions get made.
- **Example response:** "Automation is when tasks run themselves after a trigger, like a receipt email. AI is when the system uses patterns learned from data to make a judgment, like flagging a strange purchase."
- **Acknowledgment:** Saved. If your two sentences survive a friend's follow-up question, they're yours for good.

### P1-REF-006 — A question for a vendor
- **Placement:** after the combined-systems passage or in the completion area.
- **Prompt:** A vendor tells you their product is "AI-powered." Write the one question you'd ask them.
- **Response guidance:** Strong questions ask about mechanism or data — what was learned, from what, to do which job?
- **Example response:** "Which specific feature uses AI, and what does the system learn from to make that work?"
- **Acknowledgment:** Saved. A question like that moves the conversation from labels to evidence.

### P1-REF-007 — A "not enough information" moment
- **Placement:** after scenario 10's explanation in the activity, or in the completion area.
- **Prompt:** Describe a product description you've seen — an ad, an app store page — where you genuinely couldn't tell whether AI was involved. What was missing?
- **Response guidance:** What was described (the interface? the promise?) and what wasn't (the mechanism)?
- **Example response:** "A budgeting app that 'intelligently organizes your spending.' It described the result, never the method — categories could be keyword rules or learned patterns."
- **Acknowledgment:** Saved. Naming what's missing is the most transferable move in this section.
