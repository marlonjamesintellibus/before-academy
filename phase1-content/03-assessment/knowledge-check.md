## Introduction (learner-facing)

**Quick knowledge check - practice, not graded**

Four questions to try the ideas out before the assessment. Feedback is immediate, nothing is scored, and a wrong answer hands you exactly the right thing to review.

## Questions

### P1-KC-001-Q01 - definition *(LO1–LO3 · foundational · kind=check)*

**Which of these best describes artificial intelligence?**

- A) Any software that runs without human effort
- B) Systems that find patterns in data to classify, predict, or generate things *(correct)*
- C) Software with an unusually large number of rules
- D) Any product described as "smart"

**Correct:** Correct. Patterns learned from data - instead of only written rules - is the whole definition at this level.
**Incorrect:** Not quite - effort, rule count, and labels don't define AI. The defining move is using patterns learned from data to classify, predict, or generate. *(Chip: Review - Artificial intelligence.)*

### P1-KC-001-Q02 - automation vs AI *(LO4, LO5 · applied · kind=check · misconception M2)*

**A report generates itself every Monday morning and emails the team. Which is accurate?**

- A) It's automated, so it uses AI
- B) It's automated, and nothing described involves AI *(correct)*
- C) It uses AI, so it can't be called automation
- D) It's neither - reports are documents, not systems

**Correct:** Correct. The clue is "every Monday" - a schedule running a fixed process. Automation and AI are separate questions, and no learned step is described here.
**Incorrect:** Not quite - a schedule firing a fixed process is automation, full stop. Automation neither implies nor excludes AI; you check for a learned judgment separately, and this description has none. *(Chip: Review - Automation.)*

### P1-KC-001-Q03 - classification *(LO7 · applied · kind=check)*

**A photo app groups your pictures by the faces in them. Best label?**

Options (fixed order): Traditional software · Automation · AI-assisted *(correct)* · Combination · Not enough information

**Correct:** Correct. Recognizing faces in varied photos is pattern recognition - no written rule lists every face.
**Incorrect:** Not quite - the clue is the task itself: identifying faces across endlessly varied images can't be written as a fixed rule list. Recognition is a learned-pattern job. *(Chip: Review - Artificial intelligence.)*

### P1-KC-001-Q04 - misconception rejection *(LO8 · applied · kind=check · misconception M1)*

**"This scheduling tool handles 40 time zones and every public holiday on Earth - it has to be AI." What's wrong with this reasoning?**

- A) Nothing - that level of complexity requires AI
- B) It confuses complexity with mechanism: time zones and holidays are stored rules *(correct)*
- C) It's wrong because scheduling tools can't use AI
- D) It's wrong because AI only exists in chatbots

**Correct:** Correct. Time zones and holiday calendars are exactly the kind of thing people write down - many rules, still rules.
**Incorrect:** Not quite - the flaw is treating "impressive" as evidence. Stored time zones and holiday lists are written rules at scale; and AI is neither required by complexity nor limited to chatbots. *(Chip: Review - the misconception callout.)*
