## Final learning outcomes (canonical — do not renumber)

After the section, the learner can:

- **LO1** — Describe traditional software as software that follows explicitly programmed rules.
- **LO2** — Describe automation as technology that completes or coordinates tasks with reduced manual effort.
- **LO3** — Describe AI as a broad category of systems that identify patterns, predict, classify, or generate outputs.
- **LO4** — Explain that automation and AI are not mutually exclusive.
- **LO5** — Recognize that some automated systems use AI and others do not.
- **LO6** — Distinguish predictable rule-based outputs from probabilistic, pattern-based outputs.
- **LO7** — Classify familiar systems as traditional software, automation, AI-assisted, or a combination.
- **LO8** — Explain why a system should not be labelled AI just because it seems advanced.
- **LO9** — Identify when there is not enough information to classify a system.
- **LO10** — Avoid the misconception that AI thinks or understands like a person.

## Learning-outcome dependencies

- LO4 and LO5 depend on LO2 and LO3 (automation and AI must each be understood before their relationship).
- LO6 depends on LO1 and LO3 (the deterministic/probabilistic contrast requires both poles).
- LO7 depends on LO1–LO6 (classification applies all prior distinctions).
- LO8 depends on LO1 (the contrast case is rule-based complexity).
- LO9 depends on LO7 (recognizing insufficiency presumes knowing what sufficient evidence looks like).
- LO10 depends on LO3.

Teaching order in the lesson (CS §5.2) respects these dependencies.
